How It Works

3D printing works by a machine heating up a material often called "PLA" (Polylactic Acid) to around 200 degrees Celsius, then the printer extruder moves in a specific way to form the object you programmed it to make. The object is built one thin layer at a time, from the bottom up.

The Printing Process

Every print, no matter how complicated the model is, goes through the same five stages. Each layer must cool before the next one lands on top of it, which is why bigger prints take hours instead of minutes.

  1. You download or design a 3D model, usually saved as an STL file.
  2. A slicer program cuts that model into hundreds of horizontal layers.
  3. The slicer writes G-code, the movement instructions the printer reads.
  4. The printer heats the nozzle and bed, then traces each layer in melted plastic.
  5. The finished part cools on the bed and pops off once it contracts.

Terms Used In This Process

These are the three parts of the machine doing most of the work:

Extruder
The assembly that grips the filament and pushes it toward the hot end at a controlled speed.
Hot End
The heated nozzle where filament melts before it is laid down onto the print.
Build Plate
The flat, usually heated surface the object is printed on top of.
