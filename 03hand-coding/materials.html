Materials

There are many kinds of materials you can print with, all with different properties that are used in various situations. If you are just getting started, begin with PLA and stay there for a while before moving on to anything more demanding.

Common Filaments

Some of the most common 3D printer materials include the following:

PLA
The most common material, capable of printing toys, 3D models, and holders for light objects.
PETG
Stronger than PLA, used for more functional things and, in some cases, outdoor things as well.
ABS
Tough and heat resistant, but more prone to warping, lets off a lot of fumes, and can be harder to print with.
ASA
UV and weather resistant. It can warp and needs good ventilation, but it is the best choice for outdoor parts, some automotive parts, and signs.
TPU
More flexible, though slower and harder to print with. Use it for gaskets, grips, bumpers, and feet.

Storing Your Filament

Filament absorbs moisture out of the air, and wet filament pops and strings while it prints. Never leave a spool sitting open on the shelf for weeks if you want clean results.

  1. Keep each spool sealed in a bag with a desiccant pack when it is not loaded.
  2. Store the bags somewhere dry and out of direct sunlight.
  3. Dry a damp spool in a filament dryer or a low oven before printing with it.
  4. Label every spool with the material type and the date you opened it.
