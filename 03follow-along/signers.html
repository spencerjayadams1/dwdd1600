Who Were the Signers

Famous Signers

The United States Declaration of Independence had 56 signers, and several became major figures in American history. A few of the most famous are

John Hancock
President of the Continental Congress and one of the most recognizable signers of the Declaration. His enormous signature made his name synonymous with signing your name.
Benjamin Franklin
Scientist, inventor, diplomat, printer, and Founding Father. He was already internationally famous by 1776 and was one of the oldest delegates to sign.
Thomas Jefferson
The principal author of the Declaration of Independence. He was only 33 when he drafted it and later became the third President of the United States.
John Adams
A major advocate for independence and later the second President of the United States. He was one of the Declaration's strongest political champions.
Samuel Adams
A prominent Massachusetts revolutionary and cousin of John Adams. He was a major figure in revolutionary politics before and during the American Revolution.
John Witherspoon
A Scottish-American minister and president of the College of New Jersey, now Princeton University. He was the only active clergyman to sign the Declaration.
Charles Carroll of Carrollton
One of the wealthiest men in the colonies and the last surviving signer of the Declaration. He died in 1832.

Jefferson, Franklin, and Adams are often associated with the Declaration so strongly that it’s easy to forget that the document ultimately had 56 signatures. It wasn’t just a handful of famous founders sitting around writing it. It was a pretty substantial coalition of delegates representing the thirteen colonies.
